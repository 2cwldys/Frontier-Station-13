====================================================================
 PLAYER-BUILT SHUTTLES -- SYSTEM OVERVIEW AND FULL PROCESS DISCLOSURE
====================================================================

This document explains the player-built shuttle system end to end: what
it is, how it extends the existing Drydock ship system (see
docs/drydock_ships_system.txt -- read that first if you haven't; this
document assumes you already know how a normal drydock ship works), and
exactly what a player does to build, commission, and dock one. It
assumes no prior context beyond that.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS SYSTEM IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Every drydock ship used to have to start life as a hand-authored .dmm
map file. This system adds a second path: a player physically builds a
small hull out of ordinary construction and cargo-ordered parts, then
"commissions" it at a dedicated console for a flat fee. Commissioning
captures whatever was built and turns it into a completely normal,
independently-owned drydock ship -- its own database ledger row, its
own dedicated Z-level, its own schematic, stash/retrieve/rename/crew,
all identical to a ship bought from the catalog. The only things that
are different are where the hull came from (a player built it, not a
mapper) and how big it's allowed to be (capped at 9x9 tiles, so it
reliably fits through a station-side dock or another ship's hangar).

This system also adds two general-purpose docking concepts usable by
any ship, not just player-built ones: a docking beacon (or a sub-ship's
hangar berth) is always restricted to small craft only, so a full-size
warship can never claim a slot meant for something shuttle-sized; and
an optional facing check (section 3) so a beacon can require an
approaching ship's own airlock to actually be oriented to meet it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. SETTING UP A DOCK -- THE DOCKING BEACON
--------------------------------------------------------------------

A "docking beacon" (existing machine, cargo-ordered, wrench to secure
then screwdriver to activate) marks a spot any ship's own navigation
console can find and fly to -- this predates player-built shuttles
entirely and works exactly the same way it always has for a template
ship.

What's new: every docking beacon is now unconditionally capped to 9x9
tiles or smaller (SUBSHIP_FOOTPRINT_X/Y) -- a ship any bigger than that
will simply never see it offered as a destination on its own console;
the beacon is invisible to it, not just refused on arrival. There is no
toggle for this -- a docking beacon is a shuttle/sub-ship dock, full
stop. (A full-size drydock ship was never able to physically benefit
from docking at one anyway -- every ship, of any size, already has its
own permanently separate Z-level regardless of where its marker is
parked.) This is the piece that lets a beacon placed right at a
player-built airlock cycler's exterior door only ever be claimed by
something the cycler was actually sized for.

Placement matters: put the beacon exactly where you want a ship's hull
to end up sitting once docked -- landing is still guaranteed never to
destroy or overwrite anything already there (this was already true of
every docking beacon; nothing new here).

The beacon is flush with the floor and never blocks movement, so it can
sit directly underneath an airlock cycler's exterior door -- the door
opens and closes over it normally, and visually covers its icon
whenever it's shut. Persistence doesn't care about any of this either;
it saves/restores by position exactly like every other tracked object,
regardless of what door or machine happens to share its tile.

A multitool rotates a beacon's facing -- see section 3 for why that
matters, and note it also moves the build envelope itself (section 5).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE DOCKING TRANSPONDER -- MATCHING AN AIRLOCK'S FACING
--------------------------------------------------------------------

A "docking transponder" (new, cargo-ordered, looks like a holopad with
a dark blackish-orange tint) is a flush floor fixture, same as the
beacon above -- it doesn't block movement and can be mounted directly
underneath an airlock without stopping the door from opening or
closing (and is hidden from view whenever that door is shut). Mount
one at your shuttle's own airlock/exit tile, inside the build
envelope, before you commission.

A transponder declares which way that airlock faces. A docking beacon
only accepts a ship carrying one if the transponder's facing is the
EXACT OPPOSITE of the beacon's own -- the same way two real airlocks
meet each other (beacon facing west + transponder facing east = a
match). Rotate either device with a multitool at any time, anchored or
not, to change its facing.

This does NOT physically rotate your ship's hull -- nothing in this
game, template ship or player-built, has ever had its hull rotated to
match a landing target, and this system doesn't change that. It's a
compatibility gate layered on top of the existing size/turf-safety
checks: a beacon your transponder doesn't match simply isn't offered
as a destination, the same way an oversized ship never sees a beacon
at all.

Because docking is a real, physical move (section 6), this gate isn't
just cosmetic -- it's what guarantees your airlock actually lines up
walkably with the beacon's own airlock/cycler once you're really
sitting there, the same way two real docking ports have to face each
other to connect.

The transponder is REQUIRED to commission a ship in the first place
(section 4) -- it isn't just a nicety for docking elsewhere later. This
is what ties the whole system together: the beacon marks the dock, the
console builds and commissions, and the transponder proves your hull's
own airlock is actually oriented to meet that specific beacon before
it's allowed to become a real ship. Once commissioned, the same
transponder travels with the ship and is checked again by every OTHER
size-restricted beacon it later tries to dock at.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SETTING UP A SHIPYARD -- THE COMMISSIONING CONSOLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Cargo-order three things (engineering category):

  * "Ship Commissioning Console Crate" -- one ready-to-place console.
    This is the one you actually interact with to preview and
    commission. It is NEVER consumed by a successful commission --
    it's a permanent shipyard fixture, reusable for as many hulls as
    anyone ever builds at that spot.

  * "Shuttle Control Console Crate" -- one ready-to-place shuttle
    control console. This needs to end up INSIDE the hull you build --
    without one, the finished ship has no console and can't be flown.

  * "Docking Transponder Crate" -- see section 3. Also needs to end up
    inside the hull, at your airlock.

You'll also need an APC (ordinary construction, not a new crate --
frame, board, power cell, exactly like building one anywhere else) and
at least one power cable run to it. Without one, nothing aboard has
power. (Everything else a ship might want -- an engine, a shield
generator, weapons, life support -- was already ordinary
cargo-orderable equipment before this system existed; nothing new was
needed for those.)

Wrench the commissioning console down anywhere within 12 tiles of an
active docking beacon. It doesn't need to be inside the build
envelope itself -- it's common to put it in a pressurized control room
a short distance from the actual airlock/beacon, "past the interior
cycler."

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. BUILDING AND COMMISSIONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Interact with the commissioning console -> "Preview Build
     Envelope". It finds the nearest active beacon and highlights a
     9x9 block of tiles for 10 seconds -- this is the absolute maximum
     size your hull can be. The box sits flush against the beacon and
     extends out in whatever direction the beacon is CURRENTLY facing
     (rotate the beacon with a multitool beforehand to choose north,
     south, east, or west). One edge of the box -- the one touching the
     beacon -- is tinted a distinct color: that's exactly where your
     airlock and transponder need to go. You can preview as many times
     as you like while you work, including after rotating the beacon to
     see the box move.

     If the highlighted box is completely empty -- no walls, objects,
     or anyone standing in it -- "Generate Build Floor" lights up.
     Click it to fill every open-space tile in the box with plain
     unfinished subfloor (plating) in one go, so you don't have to lay
     81 individual floor tiles by hand before you can start walling it
     in. It never touches anything already there -- an already-floored
     tile is left exactly as it is -- and it re-checks the box itself
     the moment you click, so it can't ever overwrite something someone
     placed a second before you pressed it. This step is entirely
     optional -- you can just build your own floor from scratch instead
     if you'd rather.

  2. Build your hull inside that highlighted box: walls, floor, an
     airtight seal (every tile in the box must be real, pressurized
     floor when you commission -- no gaps to space), your engine and
     life support, the shuttle control console, an APC with cabling,
     and a docking transponder mounted on the tinted edge, rotated
     (multitool) to face back toward the beacon.

  3. Interact with the commissioning console again. Choose where the
     ship ends up on deployment -- staying docked right at the beacon,
     or launching into nearby open space instead -- neither is forced,
     it's a checkbox right above the Commission buttons. "Staying
     docked" is a real, physical placement, not a label: your hull
     genuinely materializes on the station's own level, right at the
     beacon, exactly as if you'd flown it there and landed yourself --
     walk between the station and the ship through a real airlock, no
     teleporting involved. Then choose
     personal or (if you're wearing a faction ID) faction funding.
     Both funding options are greyed out, with an on-screen reason,
     if: anyone -- dead or alive, including someone connected via a
     neural lace -- is still standing anywhere in the build envelope;
     no shuttle control console is found (a hull with no console could
     never be flown); no APC is found (nothing aboard would have
     power); no transponder is found; or the transponder isn't facing
     the beacon. Fix whichever applies and the buttons re-enable live,
     no need to reopen the UI. Otherwise you'll be asked to name the
     ship (it must not already be in use by another ship, stashed or
     deployed), then charged:

       - Personal: 100,000cr from your own linked bank account.
       - Faction: 100,000cr from the faction account, but ONLY if
         you hold COMMAND rank in that faction (rank 2 -- one step
         above ordinary officer), or are the faction's CEO or
         designated leader. An ordinary officer cannot authorize this
         on the faction's behalf.

  4. On success, everything inside the envelope (except the beacon
     and the commissioning console themselves, which never move) is
     lifted onto a brand new, dedicated Z-level -- the ship's own
     private interior, exactly like any other drydock ship's. The
     build site itself is wiped back to plain station floor, so
     nothing is left behind. Your APC and any cabling/atmos pipes you
     laid down are explicitly re-initialized on arrival (power grid
     and pipe networks rebuilt, not just moved and left dark) -- your
     engine, life support, lights, and consoles come up live, not just
     physically present. The ship's own interior area is named after
     the ship, and the APC's name (which always mirrors its area's
     name) follows automatically. You're handed a ship schematic, and
     the ship is already deployed -- either docked right at the beacon
     or out in nearby open space, whichever you chose in step 3 -- no
     separate "retrieve" step either way. From this point on it behaves in every
     respect like a ship bought from the Drydock catalog: same icon,
     same stash/retrieve/crew/scuttle tooling, same persistence. The
     only thing commissioning itself doesn't set is a class
     designation (it starts as a generic "SHU") -- use the schematic's
     own Rename Ship option afterward to set one, same as any other
     ship, and it updates immediately, no retrieve needed.

A failed commission (unsealed hull, no beacon in range, envelope
occupied, no console, no APC, no/misaligned transponder, insufficient
funds, insufficient rank, name already taken) charges nothing and
leaves your build untouched -- fix the problem and try again. Two Commission clicks fired
back to back (impatience, or two people at two consoles) can't both
succeed either -- the second one is queued and re-validated fresh once
the first finishes, the same serialization Retrieve/Stash already use,
so a build can never be captured into two ships at once.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHY THIS IS SAFE -- PERSISTENCE, IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------

First, the thing that makes this whole section necessary: docking is
real. When your ship docks -- whether by choosing "stay docked" at
commission time, or just flying up and landing at any beacon later
under its own power -- the game physically relocates your ship's own
rooms, walls, machines, and wiring onto the real tiles at that beacon.
Your ship's own private Z-level (the one it's always had, reserved for
it since commission or retrieve) goes back to being empty open space
for as long as you're docked. Undocking moves everything straight back
home. This is not new or specific to player-built ships -- it's how
every drydock ship has always docked at a beacon under its own power.

Because of that, "deployed" and "sitting on its own Z" are NOT always
the same thing anymore, and that has two consequences worth knowing:

  * Its interior saves through the normal periodic sweep exactly as
    before -- WHILE IT'S HOME. While it's genuinely docked somewhere
    else, changes you make aboard (dropping items, rebuilding a room)
    are NOT picked up by that periodic sweep -- the game deliberately
    skips saving a docked ship's content mid-dock rather than risk
    saving it under the wrong place. Nothing gets corrupted or
    misattributed to the station either way; it's simply not being
    continuously backed up while parked. The next time it's genuinely
    stashed, that stash captures the true, current state in full, same
    as it always has.

  * You cannot STASH a ship while it's physically docked. If you try,
    you're told to undock first -- fly it back into open space (or
    just off the beacon) and stash from there. This is on top of the
    existing rule that a personal ship still needs to be near ANY
    secured (med-sec or better) faction beacon, and a faction ship
    still needs to be near its OWN faction's beacon, to stash at all
    -- a docking beacon (section 2) is a different object from a
    faction beacon and doesn't satisfy that by itself.

  * A clean server shutdown auto-stashes every deployed ship,
    including commissioned ones -- and if one happens to be genuinely
    docked at the moment of shutdown, it's automatically flown home
    first, invisibly, before being stashed. You will never find a
    "ghost" ship stuck at a dock after a restart.

  * An ungraceful crash leaves the ledger row deployed; the very next
    boot detects that and force-corrects it to stashed, using the last
    genuine save as the recovered interior. If the ship had been
    parked at a dock for a while before the crash, that recovered
    interior reflects the last time it was actually home, not
    necessarily whatever it looked like the instant before the crash
    -- the trade-off for guaranteeing nothing ever gets corrupted or
    duplicated. You just retrieve it again with your schematic either
    way -- there is no "dry, stuck-forever" ship left sitting at a
    dock.

Because the build site itself is wiped the moment a ship is
commissioned, there is also nothing left on the STATION side that
could ever be captured twice at commission time -- and the same
exclusion above (skip saving a docked ship's tiles under either scope)
is what keeps that true for the rest of the ship's life too, no matter
how long it stays parked somewhere.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
7. DOCKING SOMEWHERE OTHER THAN WHERE YOU BUILT IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------

A commissioned shuttle is a normal ship with a normal console, so it
can fly anywhere any other ship can -- including two places worth
calling out specifically:

  * ANY size- (and, if it has one, facing-) compatible docking beacon
    (sections 2-3) -- built by anyone, anywhere, not just the one it
    was commissioned at.

  * AN OPEN HANGAR SLOT aboard another ship. Some larger hulls (Xanu
    Frigate's fighter/boarder bays, etc.) carry their own permanently
    bound small craft. Whenever that bound craft isn't physically
    sitting in its bay right now -- destroyed, out flying, or never
    spawned that round -- the same berth opens up as a normal,
    size-gated destination any outside ship's console can find and
    claim. The moment the bound craft comes home again, the slot
    closes back up automatically. No map was edited to make this
    work; it's a live occupancy check every time a ship's console asks
    "where can I dock." (The facing check does not apply to hangar
    slots -- flying into an internal bay isn't an airlock-to-airlock
    concept the same way.)

Docking "with" another ship this way (either direction) puts a hard
lock on stashing until you undock: a ship cannot stash while another
ship is docked inside it, and a ship cannot stash while it's docked
inside someone else's. Neither ship is ever destroyed by this --
you're simply told to undock first and try again. (Scuttle is
deliberately exempt from this lock, since it exists specifically as
the no-questions-asked rescue option for a ship that's stuck or
unreachable -- but even then, anything still docked inside gets safely
evacuated to open space first, never destroyed alongside it.)
